## Order matters

For each case, complete Stage A before reading or completing Stage B for that case.

### Stage A — free description
In one or two sentences, describe the motive, tension, or process that seems most important.

### Stage B — seven-category ratings
Rate every category:
- 0 = not supported
- 1 = plausibly present
- 2 = central

Then provide:
- PRIMARY = PRIDE / GREED / LUST / ENVY / GLUTTONY / WRATH / SLOTH_ACEDIA / NONE
- OTHER = YES / NO
- UNCLEAR = YES / NO
- CONFIDENCE = 1–5

## PRIDE
Self-evaluation, dignity, superiority, status, recognition, humiliation, or protection of a valued self-image becomes central. Ordinary confidence or self-respect alone is not enough.

## GREED
Acquiring, retaining, accumulating, or recovering resources or advantage becomes central, especially where the boundary of enough shifts or prior loss makes disengagement harder. Ordinary saving or rational acquisition alone is not enough.

## LUST
Sexual or erotic desire, access, pursuit, possession, or gratification becomes central enough to alter judgment, boundaries, or unrelated commitments. Attraction alone is not enough.

## ENVY
Comparison with another person’s advantage, status, possession, relationship, recognition, or outcome becomes central to dissatisfaction or action. Admiration or aspiration alone is not enough.

## GLUTTONY
Consumption, sensory reward, or repeated gratification beyond immediate need becomes disproportionately important. Do not use this as a generic label for every repetitive pleasure-seeking behaviour.

## WRATH
Perceived violation, insult, obstruction, or injustice produces retaliatory, punitive, aggressive, or escalating action. Ordinary boundary-setting alone is not enough.

## SLOTH / ACEDIA
Disengagement, avoidance, refusal of effort, or resistance to confronting an unwanted reality becomes consequential. Do not code fatigue, illness, disability, ordinary rest, or lack of resources as moral failure.

## OTHER
Use YES when an important mechanism is not represented well by the seven definitions. Name it briefly in COMMENT.

## UNCLEAR
Use YES when the case does not provide enough information to distinguish plausible explanations.

## Independence
Do not discuss cases with other raters until all ratings are locked.
Do not consult the analyst key or SEVEN commentary about individual cases.
