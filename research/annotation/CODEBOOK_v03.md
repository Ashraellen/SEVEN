# SEVEN — ANNOTATION CODEBOOK v03 (Round B)

Status: REVIEW CANDIDATE / NOT FROZEN
Protocol: ../SEVEN_ANNOTATION_PROTOCOL_v03.md

You receive this codebook only after your Round A sheet has been submitted and locked.

## What you are doing

For each case you rate seven working categories. The categories are provisional operational definitions. They are not diagnoses, moral verdicts, or claims about real people. The cases are hypothetical material.

You are not asked to decide whether a category "really exists". You are asked to apply the definitions as written and to say honestly when they do not fit.

Some categories may seem to overlap. That is expected and is part of what is being tested. Rate each category separately. Do not choose a single label just to avoid overlap, and do not stretch a definition to make a case fit.

## Rating scale

For every case, rate every category:

- **0 = not supported.** The text gives no real evidence for this motive.
- **1 = plausibly present.** The motive is present and contributes, but the behaviour would still be intelligible without it.
- **2 = central.** Counterfactual test: if this motive were removed from the case, the described behaviour would not occur or would look fundamentally different.

Rate what the text supports. Do not add backstory. Do not rate a category 1 merely because it is conceivable.

Several categories may receive 1 or 2 in the same case.

## The seven categories

Each definition names the object of the motive, what makes it more than its ordinary version, and what is explicitly not enough.

### PRIDE
**Object:** protecting or raising one's own standing: rank, dignity, being seen as superior or as right.
**More than ordinary:** the standing itself drives decisions, including choices that cost the person something they said they valued.
**Not enough:** ordinary self-respect, confidence, or satisfaction in one's work.

### GREED
**Object:** acquiring, retaining, accumulating, or recovering resources or advantage (money, goods, position treated as a resource).
**More than ordinary:** the boundary of "enough" moves, or earlier losses and investments push further acquisition or retention.
**Not enough:** ordinary saving, planning, or the proportionate pursuit of a real need.

### LUST
**Object:** erotic or sexual access, pursuit, or gratification.
**More than ordinary:** the desire reorganises judgment, boundaries, or unrelated commitments, or overrides another person's refusal.
**Not enough:** attraction, healthy sexual activity, or romantic affection without that reorganisation.

### ENVY
**Object:** another person's specific advantage, status, possession, relationship, or outcome.
**More than ordinary:** comparison with that person triggers or shapes the dissatisfaction or the action.
**Not enough:** admiration, aspiration, or neutral benchmarking.

### GLUTTONY
**Object:** the reward of consumption itself (sensory pleasure, stimulation), not food only.
**More than ordinary:** the reward is pursued beyond need or beyond actual enjoyment, and continuing matters more than the content or any other goal.
**Not enough:** ordinary enjoyment, hunger, celebration, or routine use without escalation.

### WRATH
**Object:** retaliation, punishment, or making another person suffer in response to a perceived wrong.
**More than ordinary:** the response goes beyond restoring a boundary or fixing the problem; it aims at the other person's pain, loss of face, or escalation.
**Not enough:** assertive boundary-setting, proportionate protest, or a brief flash of anger that does not drive action.

### SLOTH / ACEDIA
**Object:** avoidance of effort, engagement, or unwelcome but required action or information; withdrawal.
**More than ordinary:** the avoidance itself becomes a main driver and has consequences.
**Not enough:** fatigue, illness, lack of resources or access, deliberate rest, or a reasoned decision to decline. Do not treat these as moral failure.

## Additional fields

### PRIMARY
The single category that best explains the case. Choose from: PRIDE, GREED, LUST, ENVY, GLUTTONY, WRATH, SLOTH_ACEDIA, NONE.

- PRIMARY should be a category you rated 2.
- If you rated nothing 2, choose the category you rated highest, or NONE.
- Use NONE when no category is supported above 0, or when the behaviour is ordinary and none of the definitions applies.

### OTHER
YES when an important mechanism in the case is not represented well by the seven definitions. Name it briefly in COMMENT. Otherwise NO.

### UNCLEAR
YES when the case does not give enough information to distinguish plausible explanations. Otherwise NO.

### CONFIDENCE
1 = guessing, 5 = very confident, for the whole case.

### COMMENT
Optional. One short line. Required when OTHER = YES.

## Practice cases

Your Round B packet begins with practice cases marked PRACTICE. Rate them like any other case and return only those rows. You will then receive short feedback on how the definitions were intended to apply. Practice rows are not analysed.

## Independence and honesty

- Do not discuss cases with other raters until all ratings are locked.
- Do not look up SEVEN materials, repositories, analyst keys, or commentary about individual cases before your ratings are locked. If you did, say so in the disclosure form. This does not disqualify you from reporting honestly; it only affects whether your ratings can be counted.
- Do not try to work out what the case "was designed to show". Rate the behaviour as described.
