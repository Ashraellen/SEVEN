# SEVEN — ANNOTATION PROTOCOL v03

Status: REVIEW CANDIDATE / NOT FROZEN / DO NOT DEPLOY TO RATERS YET
Date: 2026-10-13
Project: SEVEN
Supersedes: v02 (review candidate, never deployed)

Parameters P1 to P14 were reviewed and confirmed by the author on 2026-10-13: P3, P6, P7 and P11 stay as drafted; F4 is diagnostic only; Krippendorff's alpha on binary 'central' is the decision statistic, with AC1, agreement, PRIMARY statistics and bootstrap intervals as mandatory supplements; at least 5 raters. Residual instability of alpha at low prevalence is accepted and is to be compensated by corpus informativeness, not by relaxing thresholds. After freeze nothing changes.

## 1. Purpose

This protocol tests whether independent, naive readers can apply seven working categories to short hypothetical behavioural cases with reproducible agreement, and whether the categories emerge from readers' own words before any labels are shown.

It does not test whether exactly seven natural psychological structures exist.
It does not validate SEVEN as a psychological theory.
It does not treat synthetic or fictional material as evidence about real people.

What it tests is the combination of (a) the seven operational definitions in the codebook and (b) the corpus. A positive result supports those operational definitions as a usable research vocabulary on that corpus. It does not support the historical seven as such, because the definitions are operational reconstructions (for example GLUTTONY is not limited to food, and SLOTH/ACEDIA is defined as avoidance).

A negative result is informative and is a valid outcome.

## 2. What changed from v02, and why

| Problem found in v02 review | v03 response |
|---|---|
| Free description was not free: raters held the codebook and a sheet with seven column headers during Stage A | Two separate rounds. Round A contains no category names anywhere. Round B packet is released only after Round A is locked (§4, §6) |
| Free descriptions had no analysis plan | Two independent description coders map descriptions blind; concordance rules are pre-specified (§9.5) |
| One fixed case order for all raters; mixed cases clustered early | Per-rater, per-round randomized order with stratum-balance constraints, generated by a version-independent hash PRNG (§7) |
| No negative controls, no OTHER-driven cases | Control and OTHER strata are required (§3) |
| Cases were explained in their final sentence; mixed cases announced their own ambiguity | Corpus acceptance criteria and a lint check (§3.2) |
| Alpha on 0/1/2 only; no decision rule | Binary "central" alpha is the decision statistic; ordinal alpha is reported; thresholds, bootstrap and verdict logic are pre-specified (§9) |
| Analyst key in the same public repository as the rater packet | Master corpus, key, ID map and salt stay private until the round closes; only hashes are published at freeze (§8) |
| Build script header differed from the published packet | Packets are generated per rater and verified by hash; the published artefacts are the scripts and commitment, not hand-edited files (§8) |
| No training, no practice | Four practice cases with feedback between practice and the main Round B set (§6) |
| Single-author synthetic corpus only | An independently selected external stratum is required for any retention claim (§3.3, §10) |

## 3. Corpus

### 3.1 Strata (analysis units)

| Stratum | Count | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| central | 28 (4 per category) | One category clearly drives the behaviour |
| mixed | 16 | Two mechanisms plausibly interact; deliberately includes the hardest pairs |
| control | 8 | Ordinary behaviour; the correct answer is NONE or all 0 |
| other | 4 | Behaviour driven by a mechanism outside the seven |
| external | 8 or more | Passages selected by someone who did not write the definitions (§3.3) |
| practice | 4 | Not analysed; used for training only |

Analysis N is 56 plus the external count. Workload estimate per rater: Round A about 90 minutes and Round B about 70 minutes, to be done in separate sittings.

### 3.2 Corpus acceptance criteria (checked by `build_packets_v03.py lint`)

- C1. No case text contains a category name or the words "deadly sin", "seven sins", "capital vice". (Hard fail.)
- C2. Each case is 60 to 180 words. Lengths and forms should vary; at least three narrative forms (for example third-person report, first-person note, dialogue or message excerpt).
- C3. No case ends with a sentence that names or evaluates the mechanism. No sentence tells the reader that two labels apply, that the case is hard, or what the case "is about".
- C4. At most 60 percent of central cases reuse a signature term from the codebook definition of their own category.
- C5. Mixed cases carry ambiguity in the facts, not in narrator commentary.
- C6. Mixed cases cover at least these pairs: ENVY with PRIDE, GREED with WRATH, WRATH with LUST, LUST with GLUTTONY, SLOTH with WRATH, GREED with ENVY, PRIDE with SLOTH, GLUTTONY with GREED.
- C7. No v02 case text is carried into the analysis strata unrevised. Its intended label is public. v02 cases may serve as practice cases.
- C8. A pretest: at least two readers who will not be raters read a sample of cases without any codebook and report anything that seems unclear or leading. Cases are revised accordingly before freeze, after the independent audit and before the external stratum is added (section 6, step 0).

The lint is a heuristic aid. Passing it does not replace the pretest or independent review.

### 3.3 External stratum

- The selector must not be the corpus author or the author of the definitions.
- The selector chooses from at least four sources of different genres, using a pre-declared selection rule (for example random page, first passage of 60 to 180 words that describes a person's action and apparent motive), without reference to the seven categories.
- Every passage is public domain or licensed for this use. Provenance is recorded in `EXTERNAL_PROVENANCE_v03.csv`, private until the round closes.
- Passages may be lightly trimmed to length. They are not edited for clarity.
- Without an external stratum of at least 8 units, no verdict stronger than "RETAINED (synthetic only)" can be issued (§9.4).

## 5. Raters and coders

- Minimum 5 raters for a full analysis (P1). With 3 or 4 the study is PILOT-ONLY (§9.4).
- At least 2 raters with no prior contact with SEVEN. Prior familiarity is disclosed, not forbidden.
- Raters are not paid per case, are not told what the cases were designed to show, and do not know each other's ratings.
- Two independent description coders who are neither raters nor authors of any case.
- An optional exploratory arm may use language models as additional raters. They are never counted toward N and never enter a decision rule.

## 6. Procedure (analyst checklist)

0. Pre-freeze sequence. Each stage finishes before the next begins, and changes made in a stage are logged (AUDIT_CHANGELOG_v03.md):
   - 0.1 AUDIT. Independent audit of the synthetic corpus by the checklist in `annotation/AUDIT_CHECKLIST_v03.md`. Rewrite or replace failing cases; partial re-audit of the changed cases.
   - 0.2 PRETEST (C8). At least two non-rater readers on the audited corpus. Problems lead to a replacement and a re-audit of that case.
   - 0.3 EXTERNAL STRATUM. At least 8 passages selected by an independent selector under `annotation/EXTERNAL_SELECTION_PROCEDURE_v03.md`, added to the audited, pretested corpus. External passages are never edited by the analyst.
   - 0.4 FINAL CHECK. `lint --strict` on the complete master, an end-to-end dry run of `build`, `verify` and `report` on simulated data, and a check that the provenance file is complete.
1. Freeze corpus, codebook, instructions and scripts. Run `lint --strict` and record the result.
2. Generate a fresh salt. Run `build`. Store `private/` offline. Publish `COMMITMENT_v03.json`. Register the protocol and commitment with an external timestamp (for example an OSF preregistration), because git history alone is author-controlled.
3. Recruit raters. Collect disclosure forms.
4. Send each rater only their `ROUND_A` folder.
5. Receive Round A sheets. Lock them (store with a recorded hash). Only now release that rater's `ROUND_B` folder.
6. The rater rates the practice cases and returns only those rows. The analyst releases `after_practice/PRACTICE_FEEDBACK.md`. The rater then completes the main Round B set.
7. Receive Round B sheets and disclosure forms. Lock them.
8. Run `descriptions` to create blinded sheets from the locked Round A sheets. Send to the two description coders. Receive sheets.
9. Run `analyze_ratings_v03.py report`. Do not change any parameter after seeing data.
10. Publish raw ratings, free descriptions, master corpus, key, ID map, salt and orders. Run `verify` against the commitment. Publish the full report including the negative parts.

Raters must not receive any file other than their own folder. Never share a folder between raters.

## 7. IDs and order

- Analysis cases receive neutral display IDs C01 to C(N) by a salted, deterministic shuffle. Display IDs carry no stratum information. Practice cases are P1 to P4.
- Each rater gets an independent order in each round, generated from `SHA-256(salt | rater | round | attempt)`. The generator is a counter-mode hash stream with rejection sampling, so it does not depend on any Python version.
- An order is accepted only if no stratum has a run longer than 4 and every stratum is spread across thirds of the order within one unit of an even split. Otherwise the next attempt number is used.
- Practice cases appear first in Round B and not in Round A.

## 8. Blinding and commitment

- `build` writes `private/` (master hash, KEY.csv, ID_MAP.json, ORDERS.json, ROUND_SECRETS.json with the salt) and `COMMITMENT_v03.json` (SHA-256 of each private file, of the salt, and of each public instrument file).
- The master corpus, KEY.csv, ID_MAP.json, ORDERS.json and the salt stay out of any public repository until the round closes. Rater packets are delivered as separate per-rater archives, not from the repository.
- `verify` recomputes the hashes after reveal. Any mismatch invalidates the round.
- Hard leak checks run in `build` and abort it: no category name in any Round A file; no source note, stratum, or intended-label text in any rater file.
- v02's published master and key are treated as public. They cannot be used for any case that enters the analysis.

## 9. Analysis plan and pre-specified decision rules

### 9.1 Data validation and exclusions

- E1. A rater who reports accessing SEVEN materials, the analyst key, or other raters before lock is excluded from the primary analysis and reported separately.
- E2. A rater who completed fewer than 90 percent (P2) of analysis units in both rounds is excluded.
- E3. A rater whose PRIMARY is inconsistent with their own ratings in more than 20 percent (P13) of units is excluded. Inconsistent means PRIMARY is a category rated below 2 while another category was rated 2, PRIMARY is NONE while any category is rated 2, or PRIMARY is a category rated 0.
- Missing values are allowed (alpha tolerates them) and are reported.

### 9.2 Statistics

For each category k:

- **Decision statistic:** Krippendorff's alpha for the binary variable "central" (rating equals 2, versus 0 or 1), with a 95 percent bootstrap percentile interval resampling units (B = 2000, seed 20261013) (P4).
- **Reported, not decisive:** Krippendorff's alpha on the ordinal 0/1/2 variable with its interval; mean pairwise percent agreement; Gwet's AC1 on the binary variable.

For PRIMARY: nominal alpha across the eight values, share of units with complete agreement, mean pairwise agreement, confusion counts, and the same quantities within each stratum.

Why binary: the research question concerns whether a category is identifiable as central. The rating "1" is a leniency channel that varies by rater and inflates disagreement without bearing on that question. Ordinal alpha is reported so this choice can be checked.

Why not alpha alone: alpha is unstable when a category is rare. The prevalence guard (P5) and the supplementary statistics are there to expose that.

### 9.3 Category tiers (decision statistic)

| Tier | Rule |
|---|---|
| STRONG | alpha at least .800 and interval lower bound at least .667 |
| ADEQUATE | alpha at least .667 |
| WEAK | alpha below .667 |
| UNDETERMINED | alpha undefined, or fewer than 5 units in which at least one rater rated the category 2 |

The .667 and .800 values are Krippendorff's conventional tentative and firm levels. They are conventions, not proof thresholds.

### 9.4 Scheme-level outcome

First apply the eligibility rule (P1): with fewer than 5 eligible raters, only "REVISION INDICATED" or "NO VERDICT (pilot-only)" can be issued.

Falsification conditions (any one forces "REVISION INDICATED"):

- **F1 systematic pair confusion.** Some unordered pair (j, k) is a "j–k split" in at least 10 percent (P8) of analysis units. A unit is a j–k split if each of j and k was chosen as PRIMARY by at least max(2, ceil(0.25 × raters in that unit)) raters.
- **F2 missing mechanism.** At least 3 units (P9) are marked OTHER = YES by at least 2 raters. This is a candidate flag; the analyst must then check whether the COMMENT text and the Round A descriptions name a shared mechanism. Report that check. F2 is confirmed only if a shared mechanism is named.
- **F3 mixed-case collapse.** Ordinal alpha pooled over the seven categories, computed on mixed units only, is below .500 (P10).
- **F4 control over-labelling (diagnostic flag only).** At least 25 percent (P11) of rater-unit ratings on control units have any category rated 2. F4 is reported and interpreted as a warning about the controls or the rating discipline. It does not count towards REVISION INDICATED and is not by itself evidence against the seven categories (author decision, 2026-10-13).
- **F5 Round A divergence.** Among units where both description coders agree on the mapped category, on central units, the share in which the Round A mapped category equals the same rater's Round B PRIMARY is below .60, or the share of descriptions mapped to OTHER on non-"other" units exceeds .20 (P12). F5 is evaluated only if the description coders' nominal alpha is at least .667 (P14); otherwise it is reported as "not interpretable".

Outcomes:

| Outcome | Rule |
|---|---|
| REVISION INDICATED | At least 3 categories WEAK, or any of F1, F2, F3, F5 holds (F4 is diagnostic only) |
| RETAINED | At least 5 of 7 categories STRONG or ADEQUATE, at most 1 WEAK, none of F1, F2, F3, F5, and an external stratum of at least 8 units |
| RETAINED (synthetic only) | As RETAINED but with fewer than 8 external units |
| MIXED | Anything else. Treated as a split decision, never as retention: revise the WEAK categories and rerun on a held-out corpus |
| NO VERDICT (pilot-only) | Fewer than 5 eligible raters and no revision trigger |

Control and OTHER strata are always reported separately, including the share of control units on which PRIMARY is NONE and the share of "other" units on which OTHER is YES.

### 9.5 Round A analysis

- Two description coders independently map each shuffled description to the seven categories, OTHER, or NONE (`DESCRIPTION_CODING_INSTRUCTIONS_v03.md`).
- Report nominal alpha between the two coders (P14), the share of descriptions mapped to each category, and the concordance defined in F5.
- Report, descriptively, the most frequent free-text keywords raters used on central and control units.

### 9.6 What is not a decision rule

Exploitability fields, dynamic or network extensions, comparisons across strata beyond those above, and any language-model arm are exploratory.

## 10. Claims ladder

| Outcome | May be claimed | May not be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| RETAINED | On this corpus, naive readers using these operational definitions separated the seven categories at the reported reliability, and the categories emerged in free descriptions at the reported rate | That seven natural structures exist; prevalence; how real people behave; that SEVEN's hypotheses are true; cross-language validity |
| RETAINED (synthetic only) | The same, restricted to author-constructed material | Anything about material not written for this study |
| MIXED | Some categories are separable and some are not, as reported | That the scheme works |
| REVISION INDICATED | The tested scheme or definitions need revision; the reasons are the triggered conditions | That the historical seven are useless. The test cannot show that |
| NO VERDICT | Design feasibility and descriptive agreement | Any conclusion about the scheme |

## 11. Known limitations (stated before data)

- The synthetic strata are written by people who know the categories. High agreement on them may partly reflect how legible the writing is. The external stratum and the controls are the safeguard.
- SLOTH/ACEDIA is defined as avoidance of unwelcome reality. One of SEVEN's own hypotheses (a common root in non-acceptance) predicts that avoidance will accompany many other categories. Weak reliability for this category will be interpreted as bearing on that hypothesis and the definition, not only as an instrument fault.
- The instrument is English-only. Translations need their own versioned instrument and analysis.
- Five raters give wide intervals. A single round cannot support a strong claim.
- Raters are not trained beyond the codebook and four practice cases. This is intentional ("naive readers") and is stated here so it is not read as a flaw later.

## 12. Freeze rule and amendments

Before freeze, in this order: independent corpus audit, pretest (C8), external stratum by an independent selector, final check (corpus lint with `--strict`, an end-to-end dry run of `build`, `verify` and `report` on simulated data, complete provenance file). Then commitment and external timestamp, then freeze (section 6, steps 0 to 2).

At freeze, record the final repository commit SHA and the file SHA-256 values in `FROZEN_MANIFEST_v03.md`. After that, no instrument file may change during the round.

Any substantive change after freeze requires a new version or a documented amendment followed by a new round. A deviation discovered after data collection starts is reported in the report, not corrected silently.
