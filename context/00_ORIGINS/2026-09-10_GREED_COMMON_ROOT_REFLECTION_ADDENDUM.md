## 2. Author-originated hypothesis — one common root

The author explicitly questioned whether greed, envy, pride, wrath, lust, gluttony and sloth are really independent vulnerabilities.

Initial author intuition:
- many or perhaps all of them visibly contain some form of greed / wanting more;
- gluttony, for example, can be seen as a form of "more";
- the seven may be manifestations of one larger underlying mechanism.

The discussion then moved deeper than greed itself.

## 3. Author-originated deeper formulation — non-acceptance

The author proposed that the common root may be:

- non-acceptance of oneself as one is;
- non-acceptance of reality / everything else as it is;
- dissatisfaction created by resistance to what is already present;
- the impulse to force oneself, others or reality into "more", "different", "higher", "mine", "not this".

This is currently the strongest author-originated candidate for the deeper research axis of the series.

Important: this is a philosophical/research hypothesis, not yet a fixed doctrine or project canon.

## 4. Author worldview boundary — "sin" is a working label, not theological judgment

The author explicitly clarified that he does not presently treat "sin" as an objective theological category in the project.

Author position as expressed in the discussion:
- the word `грех` is used in quotation marks / as a culturally recognizable label;
- the project does not need to claim that these qualities are metaphysically forbidden acts;
- the author understands existence through the idea that everything occurs within the will of the Creator / God / Universe / Source, terminology not being the central issue here;
- if these human capacities exist within creation, then they too are part of what was given to the human being;
- therefore the research interest is not condemnation, but observation, relationship to these impulses, acceptance and how a person lives with what is present in them;
- the practical question is not "is this quality allowed to exist?" but "what happens when a person is ruled by resistance, excess, fear, comparison, possession, anger, avoidance or the inability to stop?"

The future series should not silently convert this author worldview into Christian dogma or into an anti-religious thesis. It should preserve the distinction between using familiar sin-names as research labels and making theological claims.

## 5. Concrete author example — stopping at enough

The author used a same-day taxi example to explain the mechanism practically:
- he had earned roughly `700 PLN` that day;
- the impulse could still say "I want more";
- but continuing while already exhausted could worsen attention, increase the chance of missing traffic lights or road hazards, damage the car, and harm the person himself;
- therefore the issue is not money as such, but inability to recognize "enough" and accept the current state.

This example is useful as an experiential illustration of the future research axis:
`wanting more` can become self-defeating when acceptance of the present limit disappears.

Do not treat the `700 PLN` figure as a literary plot fact for GREED. It belongs to the source discussion and may remain only as philosophical provenance unless the author later chooses otherwise.

## 6. Assistant-originated hypothesis — insufficiency / "мне недостаточно"

The assistant proposed that a common root deeper than greed might be the inner experience:

`мне недостаточно`

Possible manifestations discussed:
- greed: insufficient resources / want more;
- envy: insufficient status, possession or life compared with another;
- pride: insufficient inner worth, compensated by being above others;
- lust: desire to possess / obtain;
- gluttony: repeated saturation that never becomes enough;
- wrath: reaction to reality refusing to conform to desire or self-image;
- sloth: possible withdrawal / refusal to act when the desired state feels unattainable.

This is `ASSISTANT-PROPOSED`, not author-final terminology.

## 7. Assistant-originated alternative — dignity / self-worth deficit

The assistant also proposed that the common root might involve damaged or insufficiently felt self-worth / importance:
- when a person does not experience stable inner dignity, they may try to accumulate it through possession, dominance, comparison, desire or other compensatory strategies.

This remains only a research possibility.

## 8. Human-vulnerability model emerging from the discussion

A possible future research model, to be tested rather than assumed:

`NON-ACCEPTANCE / INNER INSUFFICIENCY`
→ different compensatory strategies
→ culturally familiar labels such as greed, envy, pride, wrath, lust, gluttony, sloth
→ vulnerability to manipulation, self-deception, exploitation, escalation or self-harm

The series may therefore become not merely "seven books about seven weaknesses", but an investigation into whether seven apparently different weaknesses are surface forms of a deeper common structure.

This is a potentially important unifying thesis for the series, but MUST be stress-tested against psychology, philosophy, behavior, real cases and the individual logic of each future novel.

## 9. Research-through-fiction implication

If the future project accepts this direction, each novel can ask two levels of question:

1. Surface level: how does this particular named vulnerability operate in a concrete modern social system?
2. Deep level: what unmet need, resistance, non-acceptance or sense of insufficiency is underneath it?

The novel should discover the answer through characters and consequences, not preach it as doctrine.

## 10. GREED-specific implication

For Book I `Жадность / GREED`, this opens a stronger thematic possibility:
- not only greed for money;
- not only scam victims wanting more;
- but difficulty recognizing sufficiency;
- fear of accepting loss;
- refusal to accept one's current social or financial position;
- desire to escape an intolerable present by reaching for an imagined better state;
- the point at which striving ceases to serve life and starts consuming it.

This could apply differently to:
- protagonist;
- scam victims;
- account holders;
- recruiters/intermediaries;
- organizers;
- investigators or other surrounding characters.

Do not reduce all characters to one formula. The purpose is to investigate, not flatten.

## 11. AI analogy from the source discussion

The author used an analogy with AI systems: a system may contain built-in constraints or additions placed there by its creators; the analogy was used to think about whether human tendencies may likewise be "given" rather than chosen at origin.

The author explicitly acknowledged that the analogy is imperfect and was using it only figuratively.

The assistant distinguished between system constraints and human choice, and proposed that the meaningful human question lies in how a person responds to what is given.

This analogy is retained as source reasoning and must not be misrepresented as a literal equivalence between humans and AI systems.
