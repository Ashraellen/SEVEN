## Research question

The book is not merely about who is greedy.

It investigates where ordinary human desire, fear of loss, desire to restore what was lost, need for security, desire for more, and refusal to accept limits become exploitable vulnerabilities.

Working lenses include:
- greed / desire for gain;
- thirst / `more`;
- fear of losing what one already has;
- refusal to accept irreversible loss;
- sunk-cost continuation;
- desire to restore stability;
- need to regain control;
- hope as an exploitable mechanism;
- `not enough` / insufficiency as a possible common kernel.

The common-root model remains a hypothesis and may be partially supported, refined or contradicted by the novel.

## Systemic field

The vulnerability circulates through multiple positions rather than belonging only to Noel:
- victims;
- organizers / call-centre structures;
- Ilya;
- Noel;
- Pavlo;
- Nina;
- protection / enforcement actors;
- prosecutor.

These are not morally or legally equivalent positions.

## Victims

Victims must not be reduced to `greedy fools`.

Different victims may be vulnerable for different reasons:
- desire for profit;
- fear that savings will disappear;
- desire to recover a previous loss;
- inability to accept that money is already gone;
- fear of missing a last chance to protect family wealth;
- trust in authority;
- urgency / panic;
- hope.

A victim's psychological vulnerability can help explain why a fraud works without transferring legal responsibility away from the fraudster.

## Noel

Noel gradually discovers structural similarity between the stories he tells himself and the mechanisms used on victims.

Possible recurring Noel rationalizations:
- restore family capital;
- solve only this one problem;
- finish one more cycle;
- protect Pavlo / others;
- close debt;
- reduce his personal exposure without abandoning benefit;
- recover control before stopping.

The key is not to declare Noel and victims `the same`, but to show recurring human difficulty accepting loss / limit / enough.

## Nina

Nina's desire for money/security must remain human and specific:
- family stability;
- return of lost family capital;
- property / material security;
- fear of renewed precarity.

Her pressure may be excessive and damaging while remaining understandable.

`NINA PRESSURE != CRIMINAL COMPLICITY`

## Pavlo

Pavlo's later desire to stop paying for protection because danger seems to have disappeared is a strong research event:
- the protection works;
- because it works, its cost begins to look unnecessary;
- the desire to retain more becomes rationalizable.

This should be dramatized, not labeled as a thesis.

## Late prosecutor dialogue

The prosecutor conversation is a major explicit interpretation node, but not the only place where the research exists.

Noel may articulate:
`Жажда — это жадность. Те же яйца, только вид сбоку.`

This is an author-directed memorable formulation / tonal anchor, not necessarily mandatory final wording.

The prosecutor should resist simplification.

He may challenge any move that sounds like victim-blaming:
`То есть виноваты они?`

Noel's position must preserve:
- vulnerability explains mechanism;
- exploitation remains exploitation;
- guilt is not transferred;
- the same human mechanism can appear in different moral/legal positions.

The prosecutor himself may have a form of thirst, but its exact form remains open and must not be invented as settled canon.
