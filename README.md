# SEVEN

**SEVEN** is a research-through-fiction cycle of seven independent full-length novels built around seven recurring human vulnerabilities.

The traditional labels — **greed, envy, pride, wrath, lust, gluttony, sloth** — are used as culturally recognizable names, not as a theological doctrine. Each book is intended to stand on its own as a novel while also examining a different way human beings respond to desire, insufficiency, fear, loss, self-image, power, attachment, and reality.

This repository is the **public editorial workspace** for the project.

## Current book

### Book I — GREED / ЖАДНОСТЬ

The first novel is currently in Russian pre-final development.

It is an adult psychological crime novel centered on Noel, an immigrant whose attempt to stabilize ordinary life gradually draws him into a widening criminal system. The book is not designed as a moral fable about a simply “greedy man.” Its larger concern is how individually rational decisions, fear of loss, desire for security, hope, opportunity, responsibility, and repeated attempts to solve immediate problems can accumulate into something much larger than anyone initially intended.

The project is still under active development.

## Why the conversation archive is included

The editor is not expected to judge the manuscript only from the latest prose.

The context archive is included so the editor can see:

- how the idea of seven books emerged;
- what the author originally wanted the cycle to investigate;
- which ideas were proposed, challenged, revised, or abandoned;
- how characters, scenes, structure, and philosophical questions evolved;
- where the author explicitly accepted or rejected particular directions;
- why some passages exist in their present form;
- where the current manuscript may have drifted away from earlier intent.

This development history is evidence of process, **not a flat canon document**.

Some transcript generations contain explicitly marked recovery gaps. Those warnings must be preserved. Missing wording must never be silently reconstructed and presented as verbatim.

## Editorial freedom

The editor is explicitly invited to challenge the work.

Useful editorial intervention includes, but is not limited to:

- pacing and boredom;
- over-explanation;
- repetition;
- weak or artificial dialogue;
- character-voice drift;
- implausible behavior;
- continuity problems;
- structural weakness;
- emotional flatness;
- accidental meta-language;
- scenes that explain an idea instead of dramatizing it;
- places where the novel no longer serves the author’s underlying intention.

The editor may propose substantial rewrites when justified.

However, editorial changes are proposals until the author accepts them.

## Working method

Preferred workflow:

1. Read the relevant current manuscript and, where useful, its development context.
2. Create a separate branch.
3. Edit files under `editor/Current/`.
4. Open a Pull Request.
5. Explain the reason for meaningful changes, especially structural ones.
6. Flag unresolved questions rather than silently inventing canon.
7. The author reviews the proposal.
8. Accepted changes are brought back into the internal production workflow.

## Project boundaries

The following distinctions are essential throughout the project:

```text
CAPTURE != CANON
PROPOSED != ACCEPTED
RETENTION != CANON
HYPOTHESIS != PROVEN CONCLUSION
TRANSCRIPT != CANON
EDITOR PROPOSAL != AUTHOR ACCEPTANCE
PRE-FINAL != FINAL
PUBLIC MIRROR != INTERNAL SOURCE OF TRUTH
```

The project currently explores a possible common mechanism beneath the seven vulnerabilities — including forms of non-acceptance, “not enough,” and attempts to alter reality rather than accept loss or limitation. This remains a **research hypothesis**, not a predetermined conclusion the novels are required to prove.

## Scope

Only **Book I: GREED** is currently developed in manuscript form.

Books II–VII remain future waypoints. Their existence as part of the seven-book cycle is established, but their detailed plots, theses, and literary solutions should not be inferred from unfinished discussion or silently canonized.
